
# How to use this site

Three things, in order:  **fill in your profile** ,  **build and deploy your app** , then
 **fill in one form and tick a box** . That is the whole site.

---

## Step 1 - Fill in your profile

Open **My Profile** ([/user/profile](https://hackathon.snovasys.com/user/profile)) and complete it:

* Your full name and phone number
* College, degree, branch, year of passing, CGPA, any active backlogs
* A link to your resume (a Google Drive link is fine - set it to "anyone with the link")

**You cannot start your submission until this is complete.** It takes a minute, and we need
it to shortlist you.

---

## Step 2 - Build and deploy

Read the problem statement, build it, and put it in two places:

* A  **public GitHub repository** . Commit as you work. We read the commit history, and one
  single commit at the end looks like copy-paste.
* A **live URL** that opens for a stranger. Vercel, Netlify and Render are free and need no
  credit card.

Keep a `PROMPTS.md` in your repository  **while you work** , not at the end. At least five dated
entries: what you asked for, what the tool produced, what was wrong with it, and what you did
instead with a link to the commit. At least two of them about authorisation, access control or the
audit trail, because that is where a confident wrong answer costs the most here.

Your commit history shows whether you wrote it as you went. "The AI was helpful" is not an entry
and scores nothing.

---

## Step 3 - Fill in the form and hand it in

Click **My submission** in the menu. Everything we mark is on that one page:

* Project title and a one-line description
* Your **GitHub repository link** and your **live website link**
* Six questions, each with its own box

**Save as often as you like.** Nothing is final until you tick
**"I am done. Submit this for evaluation."** at the bottom. Until then your submission shows
as a  **Draft** ; after that it shows as  **Submitted** , and you can still change it right up to
the deadline.

Whatever is on that form at **Thursday 12 November 2026, 6:00 PM** is what we evaluate.

# How we will evaluate you

Out of 100 marks.

| Marks        | What we check                                                                                           |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **35** | **Integrity & security.**What a judge can break on your deployed system. The same attempts for everyone |
| **15** | **Threat thinking.**Your`THREATS.md`against ours, and whether your tests actually test it             |
| **15** | **Functionality.**The ten requirements in the problem statement, working                                |
| **15** | **How you used AI.**Your`PROMPTS.md`: evidence that you directed the tool and caught it being wrong   |
| **10** | **Engineering quality.**Readable, sensibly built, errors handled                                        |
| **10** | **Documentation, deployment and demo**                                                            |

### You will lose marks for

* Code you cannot explain. If shortlisted, we will ask you to walk through it and change
  something small, live.
* A README that describes features that do not exist.
* One single commit at the end.
* Hiding that you used AI. **Using AI is allowed and encouraged.** Hiding it scores **zero**
  on that section. Documenting it honestly scores well.

## Grounds for rejection

Read this part carefully. Everything above costs you marks. Everything below removes you from the
process entirely, and the decision is final.

**This is an individual assessment.** You may use AI tools as much as you like. You may not use
another person. Nobody else writes any part of your code, and you do not write any part of
anybody else's.

* **If two submissions are substantially the same, both candidates are rejected.** We compare every
  submission against every other one, and against public repositories. We do not investigate who
  copied and who was copied, and we will not take sides between you. Protect your own work. Do not
  share your repository, your code or your account with another candidate, however much you trust
  them, and do not make your repository public before the deadline.
* **Submitting work that is not yours.** A tutorial project, a repository you found, a project
  bought or commissioned from somebody else, or a submission made on your behalf.
* **Faking the record.** Back-dating commits, rewriting history to look like a week of work,
  a demo video showing features that do not work, or documentation describing a system you did not
  build.
* **Code you cannot explain in the interview.** If you cannot walk through your own authorisation
  logic and change it in front of us, the written submission does not count, no matter how it
  scores on paper. This is the check that catches everything else, and it catches it reliably.
* **Anything malicious.** Attacking our systems, the judging environment, or another candidate's
  deployment. Attempting to reach other candidates' submissions or our data. Putting anything
  harmful into your own submission. Attack your own system as hard as you like. Nothing else.
* **Lying about what works.** Claiming a feature in the form, the video or the README that you know
  does not work. Saying plainly that something is unfinished costs you very little. Saying it works
  when it does not ends your application.

If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask your college placement cell before you do it.
They are in touch with us and will get you an answer. Asking is always safe. Explaining afterwards
is not.

## Common problems

**"The form will not let me submit."** Something compulsory is empty. The page marks each one
in red directly under the box. It is usually one of the two links.

**"My live link does not open."** Open it in an incognito window. If it fails there, it will
fail for us too, and that is 30 marks.

**"I forgot my password."** Ask your college placement cell to have it reset. There is no
self-service reset, so this takes time you will not want to lose.

**"Can I use ChatGPT / Claude / Copilot?"** Yes. That is the point. Document it.

**"Can I work with a friend?"** No. This is an individual assessment.

## If you are stuck

Tell your college placement cell. They can reach us. Do not stay stuck quietly.
